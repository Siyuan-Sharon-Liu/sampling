Describe the purpose of your survey:
```
This survey aims to identify the reasons behind high turnover among entry- and lower-level
employees and to understand what changes would most improve their satisfaction and likelihood
of staying. The results can inform HR policy and management practices across affected departments.
```

Describe your target population, sampling frame, sampling units, and observational units:
```
Target population: All current entry- and lower-level employees, plus those who left the company
within the past 12 months.

Sampling frame: HR records of active employees at entry/lower levels, and exit interview records
of employees who departed in the past year.

Sampling units: Individual employees (current or recently departed).

Observational units: Same as sampling units; each respondent is observed individually.

Sampling strategy: Stratified random sampling by department. Since turnover rates vary across
departments, stratifying ensures each department is adequately represented and prevents
high-turnover teams from dominating the results.
```

## Part B - Survey Evaluation:

Identify and describe survey features:

```
Survey: Canadian General Social Survey, Cycle 33 — Giving, Volunteering, and Participating (2018)
Conducted by: Statistics Canada

1. Sample type: Stratified random sample. The 10 provinces were divided into 27 geographic
   strata (CMAs plus non-CMA areas per province), with a simple random sample drawn from
   each. The design also used rejective sampling to increase the proportion of volunteers
   in the sample.

2. Sample size: 16,149 respondents (target was 20,000; the difference reflects 'rejected'
   non-volunteer cases removed per the rejective sampling design).

3. Target population: Canadians 15 and older in the 10 provinces, excluding residents of
   the three territories and full-time institutional residents.

4. Sampling frame: Telephone numbers (landline and cellular) linked to the Address Register
   (AR), a list of all dwellings in the 10 provinces. Numbers not linkable to the AR were
   also included to improve household coverage.

5. Survey modes: Two parallel modes — self-completed online questionnaire (rEQ) and
   telephone interview (iEQ). About 51.8% of responses were collected via rEQ.

6. Timeline: September 4 to December 28, 2018.

7. Response rate: 41.9%.

8. Weights: Person weight WGHT_PER is provided. It accounts for selection probability,
   non-response (three-stage adjustment), and rejective sampling, then calibrated via
   raking ratio to match external population totals by stratum, income, and
   province-age-sex. Bootstrap weights are provided for variance estimation.

9. Data processing: Responses were captured electronically for both modes. Processing
   included consistency and family edits. Organizations were coded using ICNPO; industry
   and occupation using NAICS 2017 and NOC 2016.

10. Cleaning and imputation: Flow editing handled missing path responses. Donor imputation
    was used to fill item and partial non-response.

11. Sources of error: Sampling error from using a subset of the population; non-response
    error from the 41.9% response rate; coverage error from excluding telephone-free
    households and the three territories; measurement error from self-reported amounts
    (hours volunteered, dollars donated) which are prone to recall and social desirability
    bias.

12. Limitations: The three territories and households without telephone service are excluded
    from the sampling frame. The 2018 results are not directly comparable to prior GSS GVP
    cycles because the online questionnaire mode was introduced for the first time.

13. Documentation: Statistics Canada PUMF User Guide (2018 GSS GVP):
    https://www150.statcan.gc.ca/n1/pub/45-25-0001/cat5/c33_2018.zip
```
